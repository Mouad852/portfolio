A full-stack app helping patients with chronic conditions keep up with their medication schedules
and appointments. [Source on GitHub](https://github.com/Mouad852/MedReminder), with a
[demo walkthrough on video](https://youtu.be/sdr5R-KdctQ).

## Why this one is different

My other projects answer requests. This one has to act **when nobody is asking** — a dose is due at
8pm whether or not the patient has the app open, and an appointment reminder is worthless the day
after.

That inverts the architecture. The interesting problems stop being "how do I model this resource"
and become scheduling, delivery, and what happens when the user is not there to receive it.

## What I built

**Scheduled work** runs through **Bull on Redis**. Reminders are queued jobs with a due time, not
rows polled by a cron script — which means retries, backoff and failure handling come from the
queue rather than from code I would have had to get right myself.

**Live delivery** over **Socket.IO**, so a reminder reaches an open session immediately instead of
waiting for the next poll or page load.

**Email** as the fallback path, because the whole feature is worthless if it only works for users
who happen to be looking at the screen.

The backend is **NestJS with Prisma on MongoDB**, authentication is JWT via Passport, and the
frontend is **React with Tailwind and Vite**. Everything runs under Docker Compose.

## Features

- Medication tracking with dosage schedules and daily reminders
- Appointment booking by specialty, location and date, with reminders attached
- A dashboard showing today's doses, upcoming appointments, and adherence over time
- User registration, JWT authentication, profile management

## What I took from it

Queues change how you think about failure. In a request/response service, a failure has someone
waiting for it and an obvious place to report it. A background job that fails at 8pm has neither —
so retry policy, idempotency, and observability stop being nice-to-haves and become the design.
